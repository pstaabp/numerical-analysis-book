Lemma 6.5.6.

Let \(f_n(x) = (x^2-1)^n\text{.}\) Then, \(f_n^{(n)}(x)\) is a polynomial of degree \(n\) and \(f_n^{(n)}(1) = f_n^{(n)}(-1) = 0\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.

We start by noting that we can write
\begin{equation*} f_n(x) = (x-1)^n(x+1)^n \end{equation*}
and use the result that there is a root at \(x=1\) of multiplicity \(n\) and a root at \(x=-1\) of multiplicity \(n\text{.}\) Because of this, \(f^{(k)}_n(1) = 0\) and \(f^{(k)}(-1)=0\) for \(k=0,1,2,\ldots,n-1\text{.}\)
🔗
Also, note that the degree of \(f_n(x)\) is \(2n\text{,}\) so the degree of \(f_n^{(n)}(x)\) is at most \(n\text{,}\) thus a polynomial of degree at most \(n\text{.}\)
